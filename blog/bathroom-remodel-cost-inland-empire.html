Bathroom Remodeling

Bathroom Remodel Cost & Ideas
for Inland Empire Homeowners (2024)

February 2024 7 min read Inland Empire, CA

After kitchens, bathrooms are the most-remodeled room in Inland Empire homes — and for good reason. A well-executed bathroom renovation can return 60–70% of its cost at resale, immediately improve your daily quality of life, and dramatically increase your home's appeal to buyers. Here's what bathroom remodeling actually costs in Riverside, Corona, and surrounding cities in 2024 — with real numbers from our local projects.

Bathroom Remodel Costs in Riverside, CA (2024)

Bathroom remodel costs vary widely depending on scope, tile choices, fixture quality, and whether plumbing or layout changes are involved. Here's how projects typically break down in the Inland Empire:

Project TypeTypical Cost (Inland Empire)What's Typically Included
Powder Room Update$2,500 – $6,000New vanity, toilet, faucet, mirror, paint, lighting
Full Bath Refresh$7,000 – $18,000New tub/shower surrounds, vanity, toilet, tile floor, lighting, paint
Full Bath Gut & Remodel$18,000 – $35,000Complete demo and rebuild: custom tile shower, new vanity, plumbing fixtures, niche, flooring
Master Suite Transformation$30,000 – $65,000+Walk-in shower, dual vanity, freestanding tub, heated floors, custom tile, all-new plumbing
The Biggest Cost Variable: Moving Plumbing

Moving a toilet, sink, or shower drain location adds $2,000–$6,000 to the project cost because it requires opening the floor or wall to reroute plumbing lines. If you can keep fixtures in the same footprint, you'll save significantly.

Most Popular Bathroom Upgrades in the Inland Empire (2024)

Based on the projects our team has completed across Riverside County, these are the upgrades homeowners most commonly request — and the ones that deliver the best return:

1. Walk-In Tile Shower (Replacing Tub/Shower Combo)

In Riverside homes, we're seeing a strong trend away from standard tub/shower combos toward dedicated walk-in showers — especially in master baths. A well-designed walk-in shower with frameless glass, a floor-to-ceiling tile wall, and a built-in niche feels luxurious and photographs beautifully for resale.

Cost range: $4,000–$12,000 depending on tile choice, glass configuration, and size.

2. Floating Vanity with Quartz Countertop

Floating vanities (wall-mounted, with visible floor beneath) have dominated bathroom design for the past several years and show no signs of slowing down. They make bathrooms feel larger, are easier to clean, and pair beautifully with modern tile floors. Quartz countertops remain the top choice for durability and minimal maintenance.

Cost range: $800–$2,500 for the vanity, plus $300–$800 for a custom quartz top, plus plumbing hook-up.

3. Large Format Floor Tile

Gone are the days of 4x4 ceramic floor tiles. Riverside homeowners are increasingly choosing 12x24 or 24x24 porcelain tiles for bathroom floors — often laid in a herringbone or large-scale offset pattern. Large format tiles have fewer grout lines, look more contemporary, and are easier to clean.

Cost range: $8–$18/sq ft for tile + $6–$12/sq ft installation (large format tiles require more prep work and skill to install flat).

4. Updated Lighting & Ventilation

Many older Riverside homes have inadequate bathroom lighting and poor ventilation — a combination that leads to moisture problems and mold. Recessed LED lighting around the vanity mirror, combined with a quiet, high-capacity exhaust fan, is a relatively affordable upgrade ($600–$1,800 installed) that dramatically changes the feel of a bathroom.

5. Heated Floor Systems (Radiant Heat)

Electric radiant heat mats under tile flooring have become increasingly popular in Inland Empire master baths — partly because of the luxury feel, and partly because bathroom floors can feel surprisingly cold in Riverside winters. The systems are thin, don't add significant height, and are relatively affordable to add during a remodel when the floor is already being opened.

Cost range: $500–$1,500 for the heating system + installation, for a typical master bath.

Is a Bathroom Remodel Worth It in the Inland Empire?

The short answer: yes, especially for master bathrooms. In the current Inland Empire real estate market, buyers have consistently high expectations for bathrooms — particularly master baths. A dated master bath with original 1980s–1990s tile and fixtures can actively reduce your home's value and appeal to buyers, sometimes more than the cost of a reasonable renovation.

Industry data shows mid-range bathroom remodels nationally return roughly 60–70% of cost at resale. In the Inland Empire, where buyer competition has been high and inventory tight, the return can be stronger — especially if the home is priced in the $450,000–$750,000 range where buyer expectations are elevated.

How Long Does a Bathroom Remodel Take?

Here's a realistic timeline for a full bath gut-and-remodel in Riverside:

  • Planning & tile/fixture selection: 1–2 weeks
  • Material lead time (tile, vanity): 1–3 weeks depending on stock
  • Demo: 1–2 days
  • Plumbing & electrical rough-in: 2–3 days
  • Waterproofing, backer board, tile work: 4–7 days
  • Vanity, fixtures, glass, finishing: 2–3 days

Total: 3–6 weeks from first call to finished bathroom. More complex master bath transformations can take 6–8 weeks when custom materials are involved.

Water-Efficient Options (Important in California)

California has strict water efficiency requirements for new fixtures. All toilets, faucets, and showerheads we install comply with CalGreen standards. Beyond compliance, there are practical benefits:

  • WaterSense toilets (1.28 gpf or less) can reduce toilet water use by 20–60% vs older models
  • WaterSense showerheads (2.0 gpm or less) meet California requirements while still delivering strong performance
  • Riverside Water Department offers rebates for qualifying toilet replacements — ask us about current programs
Upgrade Your Shower Valve While You're at It

If you're doing a full shower or tub replacement, it's the perfect time to upgrade to a thermostatic shower valve. These maintain a consistent water temperature and prevent scalding — a significant safety improvement, especially in homes with elderly residents or young children.

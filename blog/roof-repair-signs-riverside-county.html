5 Signs Your Roof Needs Repair
or Replacement in Riverside, CA

April 2024 5 min read Riverside County, CA

Most homeowners in Riverside County don't think about their roof until water is dripping through the ceiling. By that point, what started as a $500 repair has turned into a $5,000–$15,000 water damage restoration project. Our roofing team has seen this pattern repeat dozens of times. Here are the 5 warning signs that mean it's time to call a contractor — before the damage spreads.

Sign #1: Missing, Cracked, or Curling Shingles

After a Santa Ana wind event — which can gust 50–80 mph in parts of Riverside County — it's worth walking around your home and looking up. Missing shingles are an obvious red flag, but curling and cupping are equally serious. When shingles cup at the edges or buckle in the middle, it means they've reached the end of their service life and are no longer forming a proper waterproof seal.

Cracked shingles are often caused by thermal cycling — the extreme heat-cold swings the Inland Empire experiences throughout the year. Even if you can't see leaks yet, cracked shingles allow moisture to infiltrate the underlayment and eventually the sheathing below.

What to do: If you notice 3–5 missing or badly damaged shingles in a localized area after a windstorm, a targeted repair is often sufficient. If the damage is widespread or the shingles are more than 15 years old, it's worth getting a replacement quote.

Sign #2: Water Stains on Ceilings or Walls

Brown or yellow stains on your ceiling, or damp spots on interior walls near the roofline, are a definitive sign of a roof leak. The tricky part: the leak's entry point is often not directly above the stain. Water travels along rafters and sheathing before it finds a gap in the drywall, so the actual penetration could be several feet away from the visible damage.

Don't Wait on a Water Stain

Active roof leaks cause mold growth within 24–48 hours of water intrusion. A small leak that goes unaddressed for one rainy season can result in extensive mold remediation, drywall replacement, and insulation damage — costs that far exceed a timely roof repair.

What to do: If you see a water stain, call a roofing contractor immediately — don't wait for the next rainstorm. Even if the stain appears dry and old, the intrusion point is still there and will leak again.

Sign #3: Granules Accumulating in Your Gutters

Asphalt shingles are coated with small granules that protect the asphalt from UV radiation and mechanical damage. These granules shed slowly over the life of the shingle — this is normal. But when you start finding significant accumulations of granules in your gutters or downspouts, it means the shingles are approaching the end of their effective life.

The granules that have shed are gone forever. Once shingles lose sufficient granule coverage, the underlying asphalt becomes vulnerable to UV degradation, and the shingles begin to crack and fail much faster.

What to do: Some granule loss after a new roof installation is normal (excess granules from manufacturing). But if your roof is 10+ years old and you're seeing significant granule accumulation regularly, schedule a professional inspection.

Sign #4: Sagging Roof Deck

A sagging area anywhere on your roof — whether a general low spot or a more localized depression — indicates structural damage. This could be rotted sheathing from long-term moisture infiltration, damaged or broken rafters, or insufficient structural support. In severe cases, it means the roof deck is compromised and poses a genuine safety risk.

In Riverside County, we sometimes see this caused by years of pooled rainwater that never properly drained, combined with termite damage to roof framing — a double threat in Southern California.

What to do: This is not a DIY situation. A sagging roof requires immediate inspection by a licensed roofing contractor. Do not ignore it or wait for the next inspection cycle.

Sign #5: Your Roof Is 20+ Years Old

Standard 3-tab asphalt shingles have a rated lifespan of 20–25 years. Architectural (dimensional) shingles rate for 30+ years, but Southern California's intense UV exposure and thermal cycling reduces effective lifespan by 20–30% compared to milder climates. If your roof is approaching or past 20 years old — even if you haven't seen obvious damage — it's worth a professional inspection to assess remaining useful life.

Many homeowners in Riverside County have 1980s–1990s homes with original roofs that have never been replaced. These roofs are living on borrowed time, and the failure mode when they go is typically not a single dramatic leak — it's a gradual increase in infiltration points that becomes impossible to keep up with through spot repairs.

Repair or Replace?

A good rule of thumb: if the repair cost exceeds 30% of what a full replacement would cost, or if the roof is more than 15 years old and has multiple problem areas, replacement is usually the smarter investment. We'll always give you an honest assessment, not just a sales pitch.
